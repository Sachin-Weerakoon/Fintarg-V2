1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This SRS describes the overall software system: who it is for, what is in and out of scope, the environment it runs
in, the quality standards it must meet and the constraints on its design. Detailed function-by-function behaviour is in
the FRS (FRS-001); the two documents are meant to be read together.
1.2 Scope
The product is a website (responsive web application) that lets individuals and small business owners:
  record daily income and expenses and see whether they can make it through the month;
 manage fixed finance payments, loans with interest, and pawned gold or jewellery;
 set savings goals and a personal spending allowance;
 store personal documents and details, and generate letters (e.g. to a bank);
 (Business edition) keep business agreements and company-based letters under an Advanced section;
 customise the look of the interface, with dedicated Medical and Financial sections.
The product will be sold in two categories: Basic (released first) and Business.
1.3 Definitions
 Term
Meaning
Basic user
Salaried person, government employee or anyone with an ordinary income
Business user
Person who runs a business and needs company letters and agreements
Finance payment
Fixed periodic repayment such as a lease or instalment (e.g. Rs. 25,000 per month)
Shortfall
Amount by which total monthly outflow exceeds income
Remaining money
Money left after expenses, obligations, interest and savings
Pawned item
Gold or jewellery given as security for cash, with interest payable
Advanced
Navigation section for business-oriented features such as agreements
MVP

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product perspective
A new, self-contained web application with its own database. It integrates only with standard services (email or
SMS for reminders, file storage, and later a payment gateway). It does not connect to banks or fetch account data
automatically; all financial data is entered by the user.

2.4 Operating environment
  Modern browsers: current and previous two versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari.
 Devices: smartphones (from 360 px wide), tablets and desktops. Mobile-first because the target users are
ordinary individuals.
 Hosted in a cloud environment with HTTPS, automated backups and a separate staging site.
2.5 Design and implementation constraints
  Currency is Sri Lankan Rupees in release 1.
 Personal data (NIC, bank documents, medical files) requires strong protection and compliance with Sri Lankan
data protection law.
 The Basic edition must be usable on its own so it can be sold separately.
 Feature access per category must be configurable, not hard-coded, to support future plans.
 Technology stack is not specified by the stakeholder; the choice is left to the development team (see 8.3 for a
suggestion).

7. Business Rules
 ID
Rule
BR-1
Total outflow = living expenses + finance payments + loan interest + planned personal spending + savings.
BR-2
If total outflow exceeds income the difference is a shortfall and must be shown with a warning.
BR-3
Money borrowed to cover a shortfall is a loan; its interest reduces future remaining money.
BR-4
Daily savings target x days in month = monthly target (Rs. 1,000 x 30 = Rs. 30,000).
BR-5
A savings goal is flagged unachievable when free cash is less than the target.
BR-6
Known obligations due next month (finance payment, pawn interest) are counted in the next-month projection.
BR-7
Basic users cannot access Business-only features; Business users can access all Basic features.
BR-8
A user can only see and edit their own data.
Worked example (from the conversation): income Rs. 50,000; finance payments Rs. 25,000; living expenses
Rs. 30,000; total outflow Rs. 55,000; shortfall Rs. 5,000. A savings target of Rs. 30,000 cannot be met because
free cash is already Rs. -5,000. The FRS (section 3) contains the full table.
